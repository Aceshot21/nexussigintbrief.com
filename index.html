How the picks are chosen

The same fixed rules every week — no hot tips, no hunches, no hype. From the 1,000 most-traded US stocks, a pick must be:

  • Up over the past 6 months
  • Climbing with smaller-than-usual daily swings
  • Above its 200-day average — a long-term uptrend
  • No one-day jump of 15% or more in the past 6 months
  • Not one of the quietest stocks (often a pending buyout that barely moves)
  • Not on the don't-chase list that morning

Of the stocks that qualify, the 3 with the steadiest climb are the week's NEXUS Picks.

A model portfolio that follows the plan

NEXUS runs a model portfolio that buys every pick at the open and sells it at the close on its sell date — exactly as the brief says. Each week's picks get 2.5% of the portfolio; the rest sits in the S&P 500, building to 70% S&P / 30% picks.

Its value, its return against the S&P 500 and every pick it holds are published in the Premium brief every trading day. The results you see are the plan you're given.

Questions

Do I have to buy all three picks?

No. Take the ones you like. Each pick stands on its own with its own entry and sell date.

What if a pick drops after I buy it?

The plan has no stop-loss — the exit is the sell date. Some picks will lose money, sometimes a lot, so size each one so that a bad pick doesn't hurt. You can always sell sooner.

How much time does this take?

A few minutes on Monday to place the buys, and one sell per pick 12 weeks later. The brief tells you which picks to sell each week.
